# The Temporal Merchant

## Location
Raevula, the northeast section, at his stall/cart just outside The Comfy Bear inn. He is there during the day. He
is there during the night. He is always there. Nobody has ever seen him sleep, eat, or leave, and asking him about
it gets you a discount offer instead of an answer.

## About
The sketchy merchant, later introduced as Gilbert, sells pre-crafted goods, tools, and materials — and happily buys
whatever garbage the player drags out of dungeons "for a very reasonable price." He is flanked (at a distance) by
two regulars who have strong opinions about him: the Absolute Believer Man and the Skeptical Man, who have been
arguing about whether Gilbert can be trusted for approximately forever.

His nickname comes from his signature quirk: every time the player returns to Raevula after a story event, his
stock has updated, announced with some variation of "thanks to CERTAIN SOMEONES, I've had to update my stock. Buy
my shit." He does not have time powers. Probably. What he definitely has is an unexplained supply chain on a
continent that is (allegedly — see: Nare) devoid of other settlements and covered wall-to-wall in hostile monsters.
Where does the stock come from? He'll tell you: "Nare, obviously." Good luck verifying that.

**Lineage note (for me):** Gilbert is the oldest NPC in all of Chef Adventure — the merchant in the empty inn from
the RMXP/VX/VXA iterations, whose ambushed caravan you rescued member-by-member to build the town's functionality.
He has been in every version of this game. He is the merchant who was always there, inexplicably. That is now
canon-adjacent flavor: he simply IS always there, and no one questions it harder than the two guys he's overcharging.

**The buried truth (spoilers for the chain below):** in this iteration, the caravan was never rescued. He had
people once; the road ate them; he set up shop where the road ended and never left. His stock updates after every
story event because the duo keeps clearing his old routes — the goods flow because two oblivious demigods are
unknowingly doing his dead caravan's job. Not time powers. Grief logistics.

## Quests

### About progression through this quest chain
Gilbert's chain is the SHOP/economy reward family: each completed quest upgrades his stock tier, sell rates, and/or
prices. The chain runs the OLD Chef Adventure pilgrimage route (mines → graves) — the road his caravan used to run.
The tone starts pure comedy (weird merchant logistics) and quietly walks downhill into the truth, then returns to
comedy that hits different on the way back up.

### 0) Unlock Quest: `main-003`
Available when the player returns to town after defeating the Gluttonwolf Mayor — which is when the first restock
announcement ("thanks to CERTAIN SOMEONES, I've had to update my stock") actually lands. Jerald asks the obvious
question (where does the stock come from when everything outside the walls wants to eat everyone?), Gilbert
deflects with a sales pitch, and the chain unlocks.

### 1) Cart Trouble: `gilbert-001`
Gilbert's incoming shipment is "delayed." The delay is that it is inside the Pearlsalt Mines, because — as the
player learns to their disbelief — his supply route runs THROUGH the mines. Nobody sane routes goods through a
monster-infested mine. Gilbert insists it is the traditional route. Go into the mines (the half accessible from
chapter 1), find the crates, clear the hostiles around them, and bring the shipment back.

#### After effects of "Cart Trouble"
- Gilbert's shop stock upgrades a tier (better pre-crafted goods).
- The Believer and the Skeptic each get new dialogue about the incident, each MORE convinced of their position.

### 2) The Route Less Traveled: `gilbert-002`
> Gated on the ukelele (`main-004`) — this quest lives in the tool-gated half of the mines.
>
> Implementation note: the mines used to be THE biggest dungeon in the game (~50 maps) before being truncated,
> with the far half gated behind the ukelele. That half has a "dead end" tunnel that can easily be repurposed to
> be not-a-dead-end and exit outside to the campsite described below. The old dungeon's amputated half becomes
> the old caravan route. Fitting.

Gilbert asks the player to check on his "supplier contact" further along the route. Following his directions leads
through the ukelele-gated depths to a repurposed tunnel that exits outside the mines entirely — where the player
finds not a contact but an old waystation camp — crates, tarps, a fire pit long cold, all marked with a caravan
sigil. Old. Very old. Nobody has been here in years. The player retrieves what's usable; Gilbert thanks them,
overpays noticeably, and does not explain anything.

#### After effects of "The Route Less Traveled"
- Materials stock upgrades a tier; Gilbert's buyback rates improve permanently.
- Gilbert's idle dialogue gains one new line that is slightly less salesman than usual.

### 3) Where the Road Ends: `gilbert-003`
> Gated on the torch tool (Treis chain interlock) — the path north requires it.

The route continues north, toward the Desolate Graves — and that is exactly where it ends. The player follows the
old caravan road into the graves and finds what is left of the caravan: which is to say, graves. His people. The
"supply line" the player has been servicing ends in a graveyard, and has for years. Gilbert has been writing orders
up a dead road — and the stock kept coming anyway, because CERTAIN SOMEONES kept clearing the routes and selling
him their hauls. The duo has been his caravan since chapter 1 and never knew it.

Gilbert shows up at the graves himself — the first time in the entire game he has left his stall. A solemn nod,
and one one-liner of respect for his fallen caravan. That's all. Then he's back to business, because the stall
doesn't run itself. (The line itself is reserved — to be authored by the Maker, nobody else. The player will read
it as an NPC mourning his crew. It is that. It is also not only that.)

The duo, for the record, does not get it. The player does. That's the design.

#### After effects of "Where the Road Ends"
- Final stock tier unlocks: "Naren Imports" (premium goods; whether they are actually from Nare is unverifiable).
- Best-in-game sell rates at Gilbert's, permanently.
- A small memento appears on his stall.
- His restock announcement line never changes — "thanks to CERTAIN SOMEONES..." — but after this quest, the player
  knows what it means. Same words, new meaning. That's the whole trick.

### 4) Nare Or Not: `gilbert-004` (OPTIONAL — and note: Nare may become REAL — a small onsen area, tower in the
background — see story-canon. The punchline below survives either way: even if Nare exists, Gilbert's trail
still loops back to his own stall. Nare being real does not mean his stock comes from there. Both the Believer
and the Skeptic get to be right.)
The Believer and the Skeptic pool their savings and commission the duo to settle the argument once and for all:
follow Gilbert's next "Nare shipment" order to its source. The trail is real, well-marked, professionally
maintained... and loops in a great circle right back to the rear of Gilbert's stall. Both men declare total
vindication. The debate continues. Nothing is answered. Nothing will ever be answered.

#### After effects of "Nare Or Not"
- The Believer and the Skeptic get their final dialogue states, each fully convinced, forever.
- Gilbert offers the duo a "genuine Naren vacation brochure" (item: flavor text only). (Or is it? — see: the
  ending.)
